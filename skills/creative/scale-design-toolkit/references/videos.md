# Liked-video design catalog

Reviewed on 11 November 2026 for a reusable toolkit across Scale projects. The authenticated account is Osiris Medici. The inventory enumerated all **4,978 liked entries returned by YouTube** over 100 pages, with no continuation token. A broad title/channel screen selected 153 candidates; a second screen for architecture, art direction, thumbnails, lighting, editing and related terms added 44. All 197 candidates returned current video metadata.

This selection contains **58 core resources**, **34 supporting/client/visual references**, and **24 adjacent business or evaluation references**. **81 candidates were excluded** as incidental terms, music, memes, unrelated entertainment or general business/programming. The full inventory includes 620 unavailable placeholders, 353 titled Deleted video and 267 titled Private video. That leaves 4,358 identifiable entries. Unavailable entries cannot be classified by their hidden content.

## Review level and limits

- **D** means the creator description or its chapter list supports the stated topic/use. It does not mean the full video was watched.
- **M** means metadata/thin description only. The topic is verified; specific techniques, named tools or design decisions still need watching/transcript review.
- Every duration below comes from the current YouTube video API, not from the title. For example, Flux’s title says five minutes but its verified runtime is 8:00.
- The initial and secondary screens cover the complete accessible inventory by title/channel. They can miss relevant material with ambiguous titles. They are not a claim that all 4,978 videos were watched.
- Descriptions were treated as creator claims. Current product availability, prices, asset rights and performance/revenue figures were not independently validated in this catalog. No likes, playlists or subscriptions were changed.
- Transcript-based findings for selected essentials are in the [video notes](video-notes.md). This metadata catalog intentionally keeps its review-level labels unchanged.

## Start with these

1. [How Art Directors ACTUALLY Build Moodboards](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xJEYViuQifg): define an intentional direction and separate internal exploration from client presentation.
2. [How to find good web design references and actually use them](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P2_Rh7u96Sg): organize and analyze references rather than merely save them.
3. [UI Libraries for Claude Code](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zsCGS-Yzjnw): select foundational and specialist components deliberately.
4. [The one thing vibe coding CAN’T fix about your website](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RCneB_MQ7qs): connect identity, section structure and motion.
5. [Perfect Landing Page Design Explained](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GTNgiTK-ic8): use a page-anatomy check before visual polish.
6. [Stageshow introduction](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=of628n5754g): add a focused product story when an animated demo serves the page’s purpose.

The first two references answer how to choose a direction. Component libraries answer how to execute interactions. Landing-page anatomy answers what the page needs to communicate. Motion is a later, purposeful choice, not a requirement for every project.
