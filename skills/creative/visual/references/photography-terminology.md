# Photography terminology

Technical vocabulary for describing image capture. Use these terms instead of vague equivalents in Section 1 of the Higgsfield prompt.

## Lens focal lengths

| Focal length | Visual signature | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| 14mm–24mm (ultra-wide) | Dramatic perspective distortion, foreground pushed away, vast background | Architectural exteriors, wide interiors showing full volume |
| 24mm–35mm (wide) | Natural wide coverage, slight distortion at edges | Environmental portraits, editorial interiors |
| **35mm** | Mild context, close to human eye, "film look" standard | Documentary photography, editorial lifestyle |
| **50mm** | Neutral — zero distortion, normal perspective | Classic portraiture, product |
| **85mm** | Compressed background, flattering facial geometry, shallow DOF easy | Magazine portraits, luxury product |
| 135mm–200mm (long) | Strong background compression, isolation, creamy bokeh | Fashion editorial, distant subject isolation |
| Tilt-shift | Selective focal plane, miniature effect, architecture correction | Architectural straightening, "miniature world" looks |
| Macro (100mm) | Extreme close-up, razor-thin focal plane | Material texture, product detail |

## Aperture and its visual signature

| Aperture | Visual result |
|---|---|
| f/1.2–f/1.8 | Very shallow DOF, dreamy bokeh, subject floats, background blur |
| f/2.8 | Shallow but controllable, clean subject isolation, editorial standard |
| f/4–f/5.6 | Moderate DOF, environment partially legible |
| f/8–f/11 | Deep DOF, most of scene sharp — architectural photography default |
| f/16–f/22 | Full hyperfocal, star-shaped highlights from point lights |

When Higgsfield generates with a specific aperture, the background treatment is the biggest visual tell. Name it explicitly.

## Film stock character (optional but powerful)

| Stock | Look |
|---|---|
| Kodak Portra 400 | Warm skin tones, soft contrast, muted greens, creamy highlights |
| Kodak Ektar 100 | Vivid saturation, deep reds, fine grain |
| Kodak Ektachrome 100 | Cool-leaning, clean blues, editorial crispness |
| Fujifilm Velvia 50 | Ultra-saturated, punchy greens and reds, landscape standard |
| Cinestill 800T | Halation around highlights, tungsten-balanced, neon-friendly |
| Ilford HP5 400 | Classic B&W, medium grain, forgiving contrast |
| Kodak Tri-X 400 | High-contrast B&W, visible grain, photojournalism |
| Polaroid SX-70 | Soft focus, warm tint, vignetting, instant-film nostalgia |
| Digital (clean) | Crisp, no grain, modern editorial — Hasselblad / Phase One look |

## Shutter and motion

- **Frozen motion**: 1/500s+ — no blur, clinical sharpness
- **Motion blur**: 1/30s–1/60s — directional blur on moving subjects
- **Long exposure**: 1+ seconds — water smoothed, light trails, ghosted people
- **Rear-curtain flash**: motion trail followed by a sharp frozen subject

For stills, most architectural and editorial work is frozen-motion. Long exposure shows up in atmospheric/moody references (blurred curtains, smoke, water).

## Framing and composition

- **Rule of thirds**: subject at one of 4 intersection points
- **Golden spiral / Fibonacci**: subject along a logarithmic curve
- **Leading lines**: architectural edges, roads, staircases guiding eye to subject
- **Symmetry**: centered composition, mirrored elements
- **Negative space**: subject small in frame, large empty area
- **Framing within framing**: subject seen through a doorway, window, arch
- **Foreground / midground / background layering**: depth cues in 2D

## Camera angles

- **Eye level**: neutral, documentary
- **Low angle (worm's eye)**: subject looms, heroic, architectural grandeur
- **High angle (bird's eye)**: subject diminished, pattern-making, flat lays
- **Dutch tilt**: tilted horizon, unease, editorial energy
- **Over-the-shoulder**: perspective anchored to secondary subject

## Crop descriptors

- **Extreme close-up**: single feature (eye, hand, material detail)
- **Close-up**: head-and-shoulders equivalent
- **Medium**: waist-up equivalent, room detail
- **Wide**: full figure, full room, or more
- **Environmental / establishing**: subject small, context dominant

## Lens artifacts (use sparingly — adds character)

- **Chromatic aberration**: color fringing at high-contrast edges (green/magenta)
- **Lens flare**: starburst or hexagonal flare from direct light source
- **Vignetting**: darkened corners, adds weight to center
- **Halation**: red/orange bloom around bright highlights (Cinestill signature)
- **Barrel distortion**: wide lenses bow straight lines outward
- **Bokeh shape**: round (modern lens), hexagonal (older lens), swirly (Petzval)

## Writing photography into prompts

Strong: `35mm, f/2.8, frozen motion, eye-level, subject at rule-of-thirds right intersection, deep foreground-to-background layering with a travertine coffee table in mid-ground`

Weak: `nice lens, well composed`

Always give Higgsfield at least: focal length + aperture + angle + crop. Those four numbers/terms anchor the entire framing.
