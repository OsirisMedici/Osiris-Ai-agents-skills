# Slide Specifications — Pitch Deck PDF

Content spec for every slide in the standard 12-slide arc.
Each entry covers: purpose, narrative role, what to extract from the brief, and what visual elements to use.

---

## How to Read This File

Every slide spec has:
- **Purpose** — what this slide is doing in the story
- **Dark/Light** — which background to use
- **Content elements** — exactly what goes on the slide and in what order
- **Data to extract** — what to pull from the client's brief or research file
- **Visual elements** — chart type, table shape, card layout

When a number isn't available in the brief, label it as "est." or "projected" — never invent a specific figure and present it as fact.

## Slide 1 — Cover

**Purpose:** First impression. Sets the tone: serious, premium, confident.
**Background:** NAVY_DARK

**Elements:**
- Client name — large, centered, bold white (fontSize 38-44)
- Tagline — 1 short line below the name (fontSize 18, gold)
- Gold divider line — thin, centered, between name and tagline
- Presenter/agency name — bottom left (fontSize 11, gray)
- Date — bottom right (fontSize 11, gray)

**Data to extract:** Client name, pitch tagline or purpose statement, agency name (Scale Advertisement), date.

**Layout note:** No eyebrow label, no slide number on cover. Keep it clean — three lines maximum of centered content.

---

## Slide 2 — The Opportunity

**Purpose:** Establish that there is a real, large market opportunity. Make the client feel that the prize is worth chasing.
**Background:** Light

**Elements:**
- Section title: eyebrow "THE MARKET" / heading "The Opportunity Is Real"
- 3 stat cards (light cards) — key market figures (market size, growth rate, demand metric)
- 1 competitive landscape table — 4-6 rows, columns: Competitor / What They Do / Weakness
- Body line below stat cards — 1-sentence context (fontSize 11, gray)

**Data to extract:** Market size (city, country, or niche), YoY growth rate, demand indicators (search volume, project volume, occupancy rate), 3-5 competitors and their gaps.

**Chart option:** If you have data over time (e.g., market size 2020-2025), replace the body line with a small bar chart on the right side and keep the table on the left.

---

## Slide 3 — The Problem

**Purpose:** Name the gap. This is a statement slide — it should hit hard. The client should feel the pain of what's not happening right now.
**Background:** NAVY_DARK

**Elements:**
- Large statement — centered, bold white, 2-3 lines max (fontSize 28-32)
- Supporting sub-statement — 1 line below, gold (fontSize 16)
- Optional: 2-3 problem callout cards (dark cards, no value — just label + description)

**Data to extract:** What specific problem does the client have? Low visibility, unqualified leads, no follow-up system, no brand differentiation? Name it exactly. Pull language from the brief — use their words back at them.

**Tone note:** This slide should feel a little uncomfortable. Not alarmist, but honest. "Your competitors are capturing the clients you should be getting."

---

## Slide 4 — Asset / Product Overview

**Purpose:** Show what the client has to work with — their offer, their asset, their product. This is where you make them feel proud of what they've built before showing them how to leverage it.
**Background:** Light

**Elements:**
- Section title: eyebrow "WHAT YOU HAVE" / heading "The Asset"
- Left column: 3-4 bullet points describing the offering (services, features, differentiators)
- Right column: Revenue potential table OR existing numbers table
  - Columns: Category / Current / With Marketing / Upside
- Optional stat cards below: 2 key numbers (avg. project value, capacity, etc.)

**Data to extract:** Client's core service/product, pricing, capacity, any existing revenue numbers, key differentiators vs. competitors.

---

## Slide 5 — Why Action Is Urgent

**Purpose:** Create urgency without being manipulative. Show what inaction costs — in real numbers or missed market share.
**Background:** NAVY_MID

**Elements:**
- Section title: eyebrow "THE COST OF WAITING" / heading "Every Month Without Action"
- Before/After comparison table — 2 columns: "Without Marketing" vs "With Marketing"
  - Rows: Visibility, Lead Volume, Conversion, Annual Revenue, Brand Position
- Cost-of-inaction callout card — dark card, gold value showing annual revenue gap or lost leads
- Optional: 1-line urgency statement below table (fontSize 12, gold)

**Data to extract:** Current state metrics (if available), projected state with marketing, revenue gap, any market timing factors (new competitors entering, seasonal window, etc.).

---

## Slide 6 — Strategy

**Purpose:** Present the approach. This should feel like a system, not a list of services.
**Background:** NAVY_MID

**Elements:**
- Section title: eyebrow "THE APPROACH" / heading "Three-Track Strategy"
- 3 track cards side by side — each card has: number, track name, 3-4 bullet points
- Optional: gold tag label above each card ("TRACK 1", "TRACK 2", "TRACK 3")

**Track naming conventions (adapt to the client):**
- Marketing agencies: Attract / Nurture / Convert
- Luxury firms: Visibility / Credibility / Pipeline
- Real estate: Discovery / Trust / Decision
- SaaS: Acquisition / Activation / Retention

**Data to extract:** What are the 3 core pillars of your strategy for this client? What specific tactics go under each? Be concrete — "Instagram Reels targeting HNI architects" not "social media content."

**Variation:** For a 2-pillar or 4-pillar approach, adjust card count and widths accordingly. See design.md for layout dimensions.

## Slide 7 — Financial Projections

**Purpose:** Show the numbers. This is the most scrutinized slide — everything must be defensible.
**Background:** Light

**Elements:**
- Section title: eyebrow "THE NUMBERS" / heading "Projected Growth Over 3 Years"
- Line chart (full-width or left 2/3) — 3 lines: Conservative / Base / Optimistic
  - X axis: Year 1 / Year 2 / Year 3 (or Q1-Q8)
  - Y axis: Revenue (₹ or $)
- 3-year summary table (below chart or right column) — columns: Year / Revenue / Leads / Clients / ROI
- 2 stat cards: Total 3-year upside (GOLD value) + Payback period

**Data to extract:** Any existing revenue, expected growth rate, average client value, target client count per year, marketing investment. If projections aren't in the brief, build 3 scenarios from the given inputs and label them clearly.

**Critical note:** If no real numbers exist, clearly mark all values as "projected" or "estimated based on market benchmarks." Never present invented figures as current reality.

---

## Slide 8 — Target Segments

**Purpose:** Show who you're going after and why they're reachable.
**Background:** Light

**Elements:**
- Section title: eyebrow "THE AUDIENCE" / heading "Who We're Reaching"
- 3 segment cards (light cards) — label = segment name, value = estimated segment size, sub = why they convert
- Doughnut chart (right side, 40% width) — showing segment breakdown by % of total addressable leads
- Leads pipeline table (below) — columns: Segment / Monthly Leads / Conversion Rate / Monthly Clients / Monthly Revenue

**Data to extract:** 2-4 target audience segments (by property type, budget tier, geography, firm size, etc.), estimated volume of each segment, why each segment converts.

---

## Slide 9 — Execution Roadmap

**Purpose:** Make it real. Show that there's a plan, a timeline, and a sequence.
**Background:** NAVY_MID

**Elements:**
- Section title: eyebrow "THE PLAN" / heading "90-Day Launch Roadmap"
- 3-column layout — Month 1 / Month 2 / Month 3 (or Q1 / Q2 / Q3)
- Each column: month label (gold) + 4-6 action items (bullet list, white)
- Optional milestone marker at bottom of each column (gold tag)

**For longer-term roadmaps (6-12 months):**
- Use a horizontal timeline bar instead
- Mark 4-6 milestones along the bar with labels above/below
- End with a "Full Momentum" or "Scale" label at the right

**Data to extract:** What happens in month 1 (setup, onboarding, first content)? Month 2 (ads launch, outreach begins)? Month 3 (optimization, reporting, results)?

---

## Slide 10 — Conversion Mechanics

**Purpose:** Show how leads actually become clients. The funnel. This is what separates agencies that understand sales from ones that just buy ads.
**Background:** Light

**Elements:**
- Section title: eyebrow "THE SYSTEM" / heading "How Leads Become Clients"
- Funnel (left half, stacked using funnelStep helper) — 4-5 stages:
  - Awareness → Interest → Inquiry → Qualified → Booked
  - Each stage: label + conversion rate or volume
- Tactics table (right half) — columns: Stage / Tactic / Tool / Expected Output
- Optional: 1 callout card showing total funnel conversion rate or cost per client

**Data to extract:** What channels generate awareness? How do leads inquire? What qualifies them? What closes them? What tools are used (GHL, WhatsApp, Calendly, etc.)?

---

## Slide 11 — Investment & ROI

**Purpose:** Show the cost and make the math obvious. The client should be able to calculate their ROI in their head during this slide.
**Background:** NAVY_MID

**Elements:**
- Section title: eyebrow "THE INVESTMENT" / heading "What It Costs. What It Returns."
- Cost breakdown table — columns: Item / Monthly Cost / Annual Cost
  - Rows: Agency Retainer, Ad Spend, Tools/Platform, Total
- 2-3 ROI scenario cards (dark cards) — Conservative / Base / Optimistic
  - Each card: scenario label (gold tag), monthly revenue, annual revenue, ROI %
- Bar chart (below or right) — side-by-side bars for Conservative vs Base vs Optimistic annual revenue vs total investment

**Data to extract:** Retainer amount, proposed ad spend, platform costs, projected revenue at each scenario, ROI formula (Revenue - Investment) / Investment.

**Formatting note:** Always show the math. If ROI = 4x, show it as "4x return" not just "₹X revenue." The multiplier is what lands.

---

## Slide 12 — CTA / Next Steps

**Purpose:** Tell them exactly what to do. No ambiguity. No "let's explore." A specific ask with a specific timeline.
**Background:** NAVY_DARK

**Elements:**
- Section title centered: large headline "Ready to Start?" (fontSize 32, white, bold)
- Sub-headline: 1 line describing the outcome they're signing up for (gold, fontSize 16)
- Numbered steps list — 3-4 concrete next steps:
  1. Sign the agreement by [date]
  2. Onboarding call scheduled for [date]
  3. First campaign live by [date]
  4. First results review at [date]
- Urgency callout card (dark card) — label "LIMITED AVAILABILITY", value showing spots or timeline
- Contact block — bottom center: presenter name, email, WhatsApp, agency name

**Data to extract:** Proposed start date, retainer amount (repeat it here for clarity), presenter contact info.

**Tone note:** This slide closes. It shouldn't feel like a question. "Here's what we do next." That's the energy.

## Variation: 8-Slide Short Deck

For smaller pitches, initial proposals, or warm leads who already understand the context:

1. Cover
2. The Opportunity + Problem (merged)
3. Our Approach (strategy, 3 tracks)
4. Financial Projections
5. Target Segments (simplified)
6. Execution Roadmap (90 days)
7. Investment & ROI
8. CTA

Merge slides where the narrative allows. Never sacrifice clarity for brevity — if two topics genuinely need separate treatment, keep them separate.

---

## Variation: 15-Slide Investor Deck

For investor pitches or large proposals add:

- Team slide (after strategy) — headshots or name cards, roles, relevant experience
- Case Study / Proof (before projections) — one client result, before/after, testimonial quote
- Risk Mitigation (after projections) — 3 main risks + how they're managed

---

## Content Extraction Checklist

Before writing any slide, verify you have:

- [ ] Client name and business type
- [ ] Pitch objective (what you're asking them to approve)
- [ ] At least 3 real market or business numbers
- [ ] Competitor names and gaps
- [ ] Client's core service and differentiators
- [ ] Proposed retainer + ad spend
- [ ] Projected revenue (even if estimated)
- [ ] Start date and key milestones
- [ ] Presenter name and contact
- [ ] Tone confirmation (executive / energetic / minimal)
