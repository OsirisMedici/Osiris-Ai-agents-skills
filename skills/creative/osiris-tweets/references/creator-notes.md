# Creator references

Primary sources read on 3 October 2026. Osiris explicitly chose Dan Koe and liked the Paul Graham essay. These authors offer useful editorial ideas; this is not a measured formula for attention or proof of how X ranks posts.

## Dan Koe: a personal point of view

[You don't need a niche, you need a point of view](https://thedankoe.com/letters/you-dont-need-a-niche-you-need-a-point-of-view/) — 31 July 2025.

Koe argues for writing through the experiences, values and goals that shape one's perspective. He distinguishes a recognizable person from an interchangeable source of information. Interesting connections between one's interests can make familiar ideas feel fresh. He suggests collecting ideas that excite you and developing some of them into more substantial work.

For Osiris: begin with something he actually encountered, then find the observation only he can honestly make about it. Let recurring interests emerge across posts rather than forcing a narrow topic immediately. Treat this as an editorial approach, not a universal business claim. Do not reproduce Koe's wording, provocative persona or psychological assertions as established science.

## Paul Graham: clear prose

[Write Simply](https://paulgraham.com/simply.html) — March 2021.

Graham favors familiar vocabulary and simple sentences so the reader can give attention to the idea. Editing often means cutting rather than adding. Plain writing also makes an empty idea easier to spot.

For Osiris: read the tweet aloud and remove lines that merely announce importance. Keep a useful concrete detail. If the post only sounds interesting because of ornate language, return to the underlying idea.

## Our working hypothesis

Attention can come from a real situation and a clear tension; authenticity depends on keeping the facts and point of view true to Osiris. This is our editorial hypothesis to test through his feedback and actual reader responses. No source here establishes an optimal posting time, guaranteed growth or a magic hook.
