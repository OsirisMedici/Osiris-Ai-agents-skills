## Find the idea

Start from a concrete event, decision, frustration, question or changed belief. Recover available facts before asking Osiris to repeat himself. If there is no meaningful seed, ask one useful question, such as what changed his mind today. Don't manufacture an incident, dialogue, statistic, achievement or emotional reaction.

Separate what happened from what Osiris thinks it means. A proposed interpretation can be a draft for discussion, but do not silently turn it into an established autobiographical fact. Keep unresolved facts out of the copy or ask for the missing detail.

Choose the interesting observation before choosing a hook. Give a stranger enough context to care. A small true detail often carries more personality than a sweeping life rule.

## Draft and refine

- For a first post or a request for one tweet, give one recommended draft. For a request for a few, default to three genuinely different ideas or angles. Don't force threads or a posting calendar.
- Aim for one idea and a complete payoff in a short post, normally within 280 characters. Prefer ordinary words, natural rhythm and a specific point of view. Translate technical jargon for the chosen broad audience.
- Open with a concrete situation, an honest observation or an earned surprising claim. Make the rest deliver on that opening. Don't add a curiosity gap that the post never resolves.
- Use first person when the source supports it. Keep uncertainty when it is honest. Avoid borrowed creator slogans, generic guru advice, invented expertise, forced outrage, engagement bait, hashtag piles and a sales CTA on a reflective post.
- Read the draft aloud. Cut padding, unnecessary explanation and wording Osiris would not naturally say. A hook is useful only if the claim behind it is true.
- Check length before describing it as ready to post. For links or emoji, use X's weighted counting rules or a suitable counter rather than pretending a naive character count is exact.

Explain the editorial choice briefly only when useful. Ask for a meaningful reaction to the draft, not a generic approval ritual. When Osiris gives feedback, revise the actual writing and append that feedback to the working records. Keep proposed voice traits separate from confirmed preferences.

## Keep the collaboration usable

Save dated drafts, source provenance, exact user feedback and status in the private `content/twitter/` records. Label draft, user-approved and published distinctly; never infer approval or publication from silence. Keep one canonical voice file instead of adding a new voice guide on every turn.

Producing a draft does not authorize posting, replying to other people or scheduling. Require Osiris's explicit instruction for each external publication action. Do not create daily automations from his intention to ask for tweets each day.

If actual results are available, record what was published and the observed response with a date. Look for the kinds of replies and ideas he wants to be associated with, alongside reach. Don't promise virality, infer causation from one post or optimize away his own point of view.
