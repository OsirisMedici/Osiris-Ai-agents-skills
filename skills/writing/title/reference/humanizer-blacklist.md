# Humanizer Blacklist — Title Edition

Mirrors the `/hooks` blacklist + adds title-specific banned phrases. Run every title and dek through this list before saving. If a title or dek contains any of these, kill it and rewrite.

---

## Banned words (luxury/AI overlap)

- indulge, elevate, experience (as a verb), discerning, timeless, eternal, exquisite, magnificent, breathtaking, stunning, remarkable, vibrant, nestled, groundbreaking, sumptuous, opulent, lavish, world-class
- tapestry, landscape, foster, showcase, underscore, interplay, intersection, resonate, embark, journey (as a verb), curate (when overused), unleash, unlock, embrace
- pivotal, transformative, paradigm, ecosystem (when figurative), holistic, leverage (as a verb), seamless, robust, scalable, optimize (when vague), dynamic, innovative, cutting-edge, next-generation, state-of-the-art

---

## Banned phrases — significance inflation

- "marks a pivotal moment"
- "testament to"
- "in today's world"
- "in an era of"
- "more than ever"
- "the new normal"
- "broader trends"
- "evolving landscape"
- "shifting paradigm"
- "the rise of"
- "the future of"

---

## Banned phrases — clickbait formulas

These are the patterns that scream content-marketing. They register as "skip" before the reader finishes scanning.

- "The secret to..."
- "What nobody tells you about..."
- "You won't believe..."
- "This one weird trick..."
- "[N] things you need to know about..."
- "The shocking truth about..."
- "Why everyone is talking about..."
- "Here's why..."
- "[N] ways to..." (when the article isn't actually a tutorial)
- "Everything you need to know about..."
- "The ultimate guide to..."
- "Why [X] matters more than ever"

**Note on numbered titles:** "[N] Tips," "[N] Ways," "[N] Things" all read as listicle. The Numbered Promise technique (#11) only works when the number signals consequence ("3 Questions That Decide..." / "5 Mistakes That Kill...") — not tutorial.

---

## Banned patterns

- **Superlative stacking:** "The most exquisite, exceptional, extraordinary..." — kill any title using two or more superlatives
- **Em dash in titles:** zero em dashes in a title. Em dashes are allowed in deks, max one per dek
- **Rule of three lists:** "innovation, inspiration, industry insights" — kill on sight
- **Negative parallelism:** "It's not just X, it's Y" — sounds like a webinar pitch
- **First-person brand voice:** "we pride ourselves," "we believe in," "our commitment to"
- **Vague attributions:** "industry reports show," "experts argue," "many believe," "studies suggest"
- **Temporal opening clichés:** "In 2026...", "Today...", "In a world where...", "These days..." — applies to deks too
- **Abstract nouns ending in -tion / -ment / -ing as the title's center of gravity:** "Transformation," "Empowerment," "Reimagining" — concrete nouns or verbs lead, abstractions support

---

## Banned tonal moves

- **Permission-seeking:** "Perhaps," "we believe," "in our view," "it could be argued" — strip every hedge
- **Apologizing for the claim:** any title that softens after the central assertion fails the Patek test
- **Marketing-deck speak:** "best-in-class," "trusted partner," "industry-leading," "thought leadership"
- **Buzzword stacking:** any title with three or more business-jargon nouns is automatically dead
- **Generic "luxury" claim:** Hermès never says "luxury." Use "exceptional," "exacting," or name the actual craft

---

## Length rules (hard limits)

- **Title:** ≤ 12 words. Ideal range 4-8 words. Anything over 12 fails.
- **Dek:** ≤ 20 words. One sentence. Anything over 20 or anything that needs a comma-spliced second clause fails.
- **Title + dek combined:** ≤ 32 words. If the dek is doing more work than the title, the title is wrong.

---

## Adverb rule

Strike every adverb. "Genuinely," "truly," "deeply," "fundamentally," "increasingly," "particularly," "notably." If the sentence needs the adverb to land, the verb is wrong — change the verb.

---

## Banned: technique-shaped writing

A title that reads as if a technique was applied to a topic — instead of a thought taking title form — fails this filter. Symptoms:

- **The number is jammed in.** A number must support the thought rather than decorate it.
- **The structure betrays the technique.** Repeated words and a formula can conceal a missing thought.
- **Industry vocabulary leads the title.** Use words the reader would use in conversation.
- **The technique tag is the strongest thing about it.** If the title only makes sense once you know which technique it's deploying, it's not a title — it's a template.

Fix: start over. Write what the reader currently believes, then write the inversion of that belief in plain words. Tag the technique afterward.

---

## Banned: jargon in titles

Save industry vocabulary for the body. The body proves the case in the reader's professional language. The title is what the reader would say at dinner.

Banned in titles even when industry-correct:

- *operational*, *operations* (when used as the whole concept)
- *IRR*, *NPV*, *EBITDA*, *RevPAR*, *ADR*, *amortization*
- *moat*, *moat-building*
- *audit-grade*, *audit-quality*
- *positioning*, *competitive positioning*
- *ecosystem*, *platform* (when figurative)
- *leverage* (as a noun or verb)
- *underwriting* (when the avatar is not a finance audience)

Use specialist vocabulary only when the intended reader needs it to understand the claim.

---

## Final humanizer pass — four checks

Before any title leaves this skill:

1. **Read it aloud.** If the rhythm feels like LLM rhythm — even cadence, no surprise — kill it.
2. **Search for any blacklisted word, phrase, or pattern above.** If present → rewrite.
3. **Strip every adjective and adverb.** Read what remains. If the title still works, the original probably had filler. If the stripped version is weaker, restore only the words that earned their place.
4. **Technique-shaped check.** Does the title work as a thought, independent of its technique label? If the technique is doing the lifting, the sentence isn't. Rewrite.

A title that survives this pass is ready for the dinner-table test and the Instagram Screenshot Test (Stage 7 of SKILL.md).
