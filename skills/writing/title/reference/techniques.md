# The 12 Title Techniques

Each title generated via `/title` uses **one** of these named techniques. Tag the title with the technique number and name. No two titles in a batch may use the same technique.

For real-world example titles per technique, see `reference-titles.md`.

---

## 01. Curiosity Gap

**Principle:** Withhold the payoff. The reader cannot leave without finding out. The title plants a question; the article answers it.



**When to deploy:** When the article has a clear payoff that can be teased without spoiling. The title's job is to make the body unavoidable.

**Dek pattern:** State the stake clearly. The dek answers "why does this matter," not "what's the answer." Title teases. Dek confirms it's worth opening.

---

## 02. Contradiction / Inversion

**Principle:** State the opposite of what the reader expects. Take the conventional wisdom and flip it on its head. The reader's brain has to engage to resolve the dissonance.



**When to deploy:** When the article inverts a piece of conventional wisdom. The title should embody the inversion — not summarize it.

**Dek pattern:** Brief setup of the convention being inverted, then the consequence. One sentence.

---

## 03. Specificity Number (Ogilvy)

**Principle:** One provable, oddly specific, vivid number. The single detail proves the larger truth without the larger truth needing to be stated. "Quiet" → "the loudest noise is the electric clock." "Expensive" → "₹40 Crore."



**When to deploy:** When the article has a hard, specific, citable number buried in the body. Surface that number. Don't dress it.

**Dek pattern:** Cite the source of the number, or name what the number unlocks. Make the number feel earned.

---

## 04. Naming the Enemy

**Principle:** Gives the villain a name. Once the enemy is named, the reader is recruited into the war. The title polarizes — that's the point.



**When to deploy:** When the article identifies a clear enemy — a practice, a category, a type of thinking — that the reader can rally against. Don't soften the enemy. Name it flat.

**Dek pattern:** Name the cost of the enemy in concrete terms. What does the reader lose if the enemy stays?

---

## 05. Provocation / Heresy

**Principle:** Says what nobody else says out loud. The taboo, the unwelcome truth. Authority comes from the willingness to say it.



**When to deploy:** When the article makes a contrarian claim that's defensible. The title lands the heresy before the reader can object. The body proves it.

**Dek pattern:** Name the defense — the data, the logic, or the position that lets the heresy stand. Don't apologize for the title.

---

## 06. Status Threat

**Principle:** Makes inaction feel financially, socially, or strategically expensive. The reader doesn't want to lose. Loss-aversion is a stronger pull than gain-seeking.



**When to deploy:** When the article quantifies the cost of inaction. The title surfaces the loss; the body proves it.

**Dek pattern:** Name the path that avoids the loss. The title threatens; the dek offers the door out.

---

## 07. Quiet Authority (Hermès / Rolex)

**Principle:** Says less than the moment calls for. Restraint reads as authority. Two to four words. No adjectives. The shortest title in the room becomes the loudest.



**When to deploy:** When everything around the avatar is shouting. The title becomes the quietest line in the room. Use sparingly — once per batch maximum.

**Dek pattern:** Slightly longer than the title would suggest. The dek does the explaining the title refuses to do. Still no adjectives.

---

## 08. Confession (Open Loop)

**Principle:** First-person admission that promises a story. Begins mid-thought, ends before resolution. Forces the reader to ask "wait, why?"



**When to deploy:** When the article is in Osiris's voice or first-person founder's voice. Personal brand pieces, founder's notes, post-mortem essays. **Do not deploy** for client editorial in third-person editorial register.

**Dek pattern:** State the lesson learned, in the same voice. The dek closes the open loop just enough to make the body unavoidable.

---

## 09. Re-Framing

**Principle:** Recategorizes a familiar thing. Moves the reader's mental file from one folder to another. "X isn't Y. It's Z."



**When to deploy:** When the article's central move is to recategorize — to argue that something the reader has been treating as one type of decision is actually a different type entirely. The whole article is the proof of the new category.

**Dek pattern:** State the consequence of the new category. If it's an IRR decision, what does that mean for the reader's next move?

---

## 10. The Verdict

**Principle:** Final-judgment voice. No hedging. No "in our view," no "perhaps," no "many would argue." The sentence ends where confidence ends.



**When to deploy:** When the article is calling the time of death on a category, era, or convention. The title is the obituary; the body is the autopsy.

**Dek pattern:** Name what's replacing it. The verdict creates the void; the dek points at what fills it.

---

## 11. Numbered Promise

**Principle:** Bounded, specific, useful. The number creates a contract — finite, finishable, worth the read. Different from listicles: the number must signal *consequence*, not "tips."



**When to deploy:** When the article is structured as a list with stakes attached to it. **Avoid generic listicle framing** — "5 Tips," "7 Things," "10 Ways." The number must imply consequence, not tutorial.

**Dek pattern:** Name the cost of getting the list wrong. Or name the asymmetry — short list, large outcome.

---

## 12. Cultural Moment

**Principle:** Names where we are right now. Place + time + verdict. The dated, located thesis that says: this is happening, here, now.



**When to deploy:** When the article makes a now-only argument that wouldn't hold three years ago and won't hold three years from now. The title plants the flag in time.

**Dek pattern:** Name the conditions making the moment possible. The dek explains why it's now and not earlier or later.

---

## Selection logic for /title

Given an article, pick **5 techniques out of 12** based on these signals:

| If the article... | Lead with these |
|---|---|
| Inverts conventional wisdom | 02 (Contradiction), 09 (Re-Framing) |
| Surfaces a hard number | 03 (Specificity Number), 11 (Numbered Promise) |
| Calls out a category killer | 04 (Naming the Enemy), 10 (Verdict) |
| Says the unsaid | 05 (Provocation), 04 (Naming the Enemy) |
| Quantifies inaction cost | 06 (Status Threat), 03 (Specificity Number) |
| Is in first-person founder voice | 08 (Confession), 05 (Provocation) |
| Is calmly authoritative | 07 (Quiet Authority), 10 (Verdict) |
| Plants a flag in time | 12 (Cultural Moment), 10 (Verdict) |
| Has a clear withheld payoff | 01 (Curiosity Gap), 11 (Numbered Promise) |

The 5 chosen should span tonal register — at least one quiet, one provocative, one specific-number, one re-frame or verdict, one curiosity or confession.
